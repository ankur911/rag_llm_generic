# How does netherlands government (RIVM) guidelines differ from WHO guidelines for infant feeding?

Here is a summary of key differences between the Netherlands government (RIVM) infant feeding guidelines and the WHO guidelines:

### Main Differences Between RIVM and WHO Infant Feeding Guidelines

- **Alignment on Breastfeeding Recommendations:**
    - Both RIVM and WHO strongly promote *exclusive breastfeeding for the first 6 months* and *continued breastfeeding for up to 2 years or beyond* with complementary foods.
    - The Dutch guidelines explicitly aim to increase the percentage of mothers who breastfeed, noting current national compliance is lower than WHO goals.
- **Context and Application:**
    - WHO guidelines provide *global standards* meant to be adapted locally; they emphasize breastfeeding initiation within the first hour and nutritional diversity in complementary feeding.
    - RIVM guidelines focus more on *national context specifics,* including nutrient intake patterns in Dutch infants and public health campaigns such as the Dutch *"Care for Breastfeeding"* program aligned with WHO’s Baby Friendly Hospital Initiative.
- **Nutrient Intake Specifics and Policy Targets:**
    - RIVM includes detailed nutrient intake data for Dutch infants (carbohydrates, proteins, fats) and undertakes modeling of health benefits and cost-effectiveness of breastfeeding promotion policies.
    - The Dutch government has specific quantitative targets (e.g., 85% breastfeeding initiation, 25% exclusive breastfeeding at 6 months) embedded into national health plans.
- **Evidence-Based Policy Development:**
    - RIVM has conducted risk-benefit analyses and model simulations to quantify the health impact and cost savings of breastfeeding policies nationally.
    - RIVM also produces critical evaluations of interventions, including concerns about some draft guidelines that lack strong scientific support (e.g., regarding excessive crying interventions conflicting with breastfeeding success).
- **Additional Cultural and Healthcare Considerations:**
    - RIVM closely involves lactation consultants and psychologists to tailor guidelines respecting Dutch healthcare settings and parental support structures.
    - There is emphasis on breastfeeding frequency and storage capacity of breasts, aspects less detailed in global WHO documents.

### Summary

While RIVM’s infant feeding guidelines are built on the foundation of WHO’s global recommendations, they incorporate *national public health goals, detailed dietary intake analysis, health economic evaluations,* and specific cultural/clinical nuances relevant to the Netherlands.

This approach aims to adapt and operationalize WHO guidelines effectively within Dutch healthcare and social contexts, targeting improved breastfeeding rates and nutritional outcomes tailored to the local population.[^1][^2][^3]
