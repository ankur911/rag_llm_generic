## WHO Guidelines for Infant Feeding (2025)

- **Initiation and Breastfeeding**:
    - Initiate breastfeeding within *1 hour of birth*.
    - Practice *exclusive breastfeeding* for the first 6 months of life.
    - After 6 months, introduce *nutritionally adequate and safe complementary foods* while continuing breastfeeding up to 2 years of age or beyond.[^1][^2][^3]
- **Key Recommendations**:
    - Exclusive breastfeeding for six months.
    - Complementary feeding starts at six months.
    - Continued breastfeeding up to at least two years.
    - For infants born to HIV-infected mothers, antiretroviral drugs now allow for exclusive breastfeeding until six months and continued up to 12 months, under medical guidance.[^1]
- **Complementary Feeding (6–23 Months)**:
    - Diversify diet; emphasize use of broad food groups.
    - Replace milk (other than breast milk) with *animal milk or infant formula after 6 months*.
    - After 12 months, young children should receive animal milk if breast milk is not provided.[^4][^5]

## RIVM (Netherlands) Guidelines

- **Dutch Updates**:
    - In 2025, the Health Council of the Netherlands (advisory body to RIVM) is developing updated dietary recommendations for infants up to two years as part of the "Healthy nutrition: the first thousand days" initiative.
    - Prior guidance covers recommendations for pregnant and lactating women, with updated infant guidelines expected late 2025.[^6]
- **Specifics Mentioned**:
    - Upcoming guidance will cover sources of protein, reference values for carbohydrates, fats, and fatty acids for infants.
    - Vitamin K administration: Newborns are given vitamin K (preferably via intramuscular injection, with oral as alternative) to prevent deficiency-related hemorrhage, especially for those with disturbed fat absorption.[^6]


#### Source Metadata (RIVM / Dutch Health Council)

- Report (expected late 2025): "Dietary recommendations for infants up to two years of age"[^6]
- Latest details: Work programme 2025 Health Council of the Netherlands (published Sep 17, 2024)[^6]
